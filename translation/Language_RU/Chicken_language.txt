Desc_0
These chickens have a barred, black and white plumage resembling dark reeds. They enjoy roaming by rivers and hiding among tall reeds while pecking at grass seeds, acorns, berries, and small insects such as ants and flies.
Desc_1
This breed can be traced back to the late Tang Dynasty, a crossbreed between domestic chickens and pheasants. Originating from Qingyuan, Guangdong, they have small dark dots on their back and are small, fit and nimble, often seen foraging in the wild or in fields.
Desc_2
With small heads and long tails, pheasants are elusive birds that are prone to darting into nearby bushes at the first sight of strangers. Yet the ancient Confucian text Bai Hu Tong notes how they're "not wont to be lured by food, nor threatened by intimidation." As such, scholars consider them a symbol of integrity and so present them as gifts when visiting friends.
Desc_3
The Bamboo Partridge, also termed "Nigugu," often calls out several dozen times in a row until out of breath. Their colorful plumage means they're easily spotted, especially amid their favored bushes and bamboo forests. They don't fear people, and will often scrap among each other for fun.
Desc_4
Standing proud and graceful, the Black Plumage Chicken has a bright red comb and glossy black feathers. They're a free-range breed that forage on insects and wild herbs, laying greenish eggs that are especially delicious. In times of old, they were offered in tribute to the imperial palace.
Desc_5
The tail of the seven constellation mansions is the Vermilion Bird, one of the Four Symbols. Referred to as Quail-tail Fowl, this term was later used to name this bird species. Their chicken-like heads paired with short quail-like tails grant them an unconventional charm. Bronze mirrors would often be detailed with Quail-tail Fowl designs in the Han Dynasty, symbolizing the Vermilion Bird.
Desc_6
Eared pheasants have unique appearances with red heads, blue plumage and upward tails, and are fit and graceful runners that only live on mountains. They make loud and clear calls and are not afraid of people, capable of digging for food and nesting in hay.
Desc_7
These birds resemble pheasants with red legs and dark plumage and can be used to cure swellings and dry skin, also known as 'Tong Qu' in The Classic of Mountains and Seas. They are rare waders that feed on plants, fish and insects, capable of moving quickly in woods and on lakes.
Desc_8
Legends tell of a Scholar Chuzong of the Pei state, who in the Jin Dynasty kept a wondrously intelligent chicken even capable of speech. Speaking chickens of high intelligence have often been found since, known as 'Scholar Fowls.'
Desc_9
It's said that "The trilling of Sycee Chickens, with their prominent heads and tails like a crescent-shaped ingot, is most pleasant." Their endearing appearance with short legs and chubby bodies means their cries never grow grating.
Desc_10
The poet Lu You wrote, "We pledge loyalty to each other with the blood of a crimson rooster." Their call is clear and crisp, and their plumage is a deep crimson. In times of old they were used in sacrifices and vows, for they symbolize permanence with shiny and brilliant feathers, hard-edged and sharp beaks, and a most striking appearance.
Desc_11
There were officials in charge of sacrificial chickens for rituals in the Zhou Dynasty. It's told that they insisted on only using chickens from the Guo state. These chickens are slender, handsome, a mottle of black and red, and have been bred in the regions of the Guo state for generations.
Desc_12
The Biographies of Immortals tell of Zhu, an old man who kept over a thousand chickens for over a century and called each by name. In time they were sold on and spread across the land. Yellow and white in color, they are proud and smart with heads held high, often found perching on trees. They answer their owner's call: "Zhu-Zhu."
Desc_13
The Book of the Later Han states "There exists a long-tailed chicken, tail five chi in length." These were once a precious tribute for nobles, placed upon high platforms. Their tail feathers are graceful and sweeping. The chickens are valued so highly, and some even had them carved upon their tombs.
Desc_14
Emperor Huizong of Song once wrote, "Autumn frost and white crests on Lady Amherst's pheasants," a line for his painting 'Golden Pheasant on Cotton Rose.' They sport long, narrow combs with white coloring on their necks, tails and breasts; a bright-colored plumage. Scholars have often lauded their beauty in paintings and poetry.
Desc_15
Golden pheasants' combs are gold throughout, with colorful plumages as brilliant as brocade. They're said to resemble the Fenghuang, a most auspicious creature. Thus, their imagery is often used in imperial decorations and embellishments.
Desc_16
Qin Shi Huang traveled to Langya three times in search of a golden elixir for immortality. Alchemist Xu Fu offered a precious ingredient from the sea and simmered it with the local chicken, the latter which the emperor found just as delectable as the marine delicacy. He even deemed it capable of prolonging one's life, naming it the 'Langya Chicken.'
Desc_17
Zuo Si once wrote, "While golden steeds are swift and glowing, the elusive emerald rooster preens itself with a marvelous shine." Legend tells of an emerald rooster deity that descends upon the earth with a fantastic glow, granting good fortune to all who see it. Many seek it out, building shrines as offerings for its blessing and protection.
Desc_18
Certain chickens can be seen pecking at greengages and the worms that gather as they fall. Thus they're known as Greengage Chickens, ordinary with a mottled brown plumage; yet their meat is fine, tender and unique with a faint sweet flavor, perhaps due to their greengage diet.
Desc_19
Poet Yao Mian once wrote, "Chickens drink from chrysanthemum-rich ponds, out upon parasol trees." Bred in Jiangnan, these diminutive and short-legged birds bear an endearing snow-white plumage. They like to nest in parasol trees or in bamboo forests, drinking from lakes and ponds surrounded by trees and blossoms. Hence they carry a fresh aroma of flowers and leaves.
Desc_20
Stocky birds that, despite their size, are largely unable to cry. A newly-appointed governor was once taunted as being "unable to speak for himself, like a Kuaiji Chicken"; yet he simply replied, "Although quiet, the Kuaiji Chicken are more than capable of slaying the corrupt before them."
Desc_21
These chickens are inky-black throughout their plumage, skin, flesh, bones and innards. Most are left to roam freely in woods and mountains, feeding on wild plants and bugs.
Desc_22

Desc_23
Chickens with large heads, wide necks, and plump rears bearing short tails. Tufts of elongated feathers grow below their beaks like beards, hence their name.
Desc_24

Candlestick Chicken is considered an ancient marvel, feeding on the ground by day and either airborne or roosting in trees by night. Their crow can keep the sun shining high in the skies.
